Two modes, one mechanism

Pair or basket, your call

Both lock the same fee split at launch. They differ in what happens to the fees after that.

Pair

One asset, nothing in between

The coin trades against the asset it pays out, so fees arrive as that asset. Nothing is bought or sold on the way.

A coin quoted in NVDAx pays its holders NVDAx. No route, no slippage, no oracle, no market-hours window.

46 assets qualify right now
Basket

Up to 8 assets, weighted

The coin trades against ETH or USDC, and the fees buy the assets you picked. The program checks the price before every buy.

An ETH-quoted coin can buy SPYx, QQQx and WBTC at whatever weights you pick, summing to 100%.

23 assets qualify as a leg right now
Both

Frozen at launch, forever

The assets, the weights, the split and the publisher are set once and cannot be changed by anyone, you included. The creator can take at most 50% of each payout.

20% of every payout goes to Basket and 1% to whoever pressed the button. At least 29% always reaches holders.

How it works

Four steps, and only one of them is yours

You launch. Everything after that runs on its own, and anyone can press it.

  1. 01

    Launch

    One ponsfamily.com coin, one payout asset or a basket of them, one split. The split is locked at launch and nobody can change it afterwards.

  2. 02

    Fees accrue

    Every trade pays a creator fee, and that fee goes to the coin's vault instead of to a wallet.

  3. 03

    Settle or harvest

    A pair coin's fees are already the right asset. A basket's are bought, and the program refuses a bad price.

  4. 04

    Claim

    Every hour, each holder's share is worked out from how much they held and for how long. Claim it whenever you like.

What you have to trust, and what you do not

The fees never touch a wallet anybody controls.

The usual way to build this is a bot that takes the fees into its own wallet, buys the asset and sends it out. That works exactly as long as whoever holds the wallet wants it to. Here there is no wallet: the fees are frozen to a program account at launch, and the program has no way to hand them anywhere else. One thing is trusted, and we would rather say it than bury it: Basket keeps the authority to upgrade the program, so the code holding a vault can change. What that means, and who holds the key, is on the docs page, read from the chain rather than claimed.

The split is frozen on chain

Set once at launch. Nobody can redirect a fee afterwards, the launcher included.

The vault is a program account

It has no owner and no key. There is no withdraw and no pause. The only ways out are claiming and redeeming.

Every root is recomputable

Every payout is worked out from the chain's own record of who held what. Anyone can check ours against it.

A root can never promise what is not there

A payout can never be larger than what the vault actually holds, so nobody can promise money that is not there.

Questions

The ones worth asking

Why does a basket need a Pyth price when a pair coin does not?

A basket harvest is a swap, and a swap can be routed badly on purpose. The program cannot judge a route, so it judges the result: it reads the leg's Pyth price, works out the fewest tokens an honest fill would return, and reverts anything more than 5% below it or priced more than 5 minutes ago. That is what stops whoever presses the button from routing the vault's money into their own pocket.

A pair coin never buys anything, so there is no price to check. A basket coin buys its assets, and whoever presses that button chooses the route, so the program checks the price first and refuses a bad one.

What does “ponsfamily.com lists it” actually mean?

ponsfamily.com keeps two lists on chain of what a coin may be quoted in: the whitelist inside its Global account, and a separate quote-control account holding the rest with their own starting curve reserves. A coin can only be quoted in a mint on one of those lists, so a pair coin can only pay out one of them.

This site reads both lists live rather than shipping a copy. A moment ago: 1 whitelisted and 93 through quote-control.

What happens outside US market hours?

Share prices only publish while the market is open, so a basket holding tokenised shares buys during market hours. Nothing is lost: the fees keep piling up in the vault and are spent on the next pass. A pair coin is never affected.

What if the keeper disappears?

Every instruction that moves money is permissionless. distribute_creator_fees_v2, settle and harvest can be pressed by anyone, and the presser is paid 1% for the trouble. Anyone can run a keeper; the payout rule is in the docs and the verifier ships with it.

Working out who is owed what is the one job not open to everyone, because it decides who gets paid. Everything else, moving the fees and buying the assets, anyone can press and gets paid a fee for doing it.root_publisher can do it, and the coin's creator can rotate that address. If nobody ever publishes another root, redeem is still there: it burns coin for a pro-rata slice of everything the vault holds, and it depends on no publisher at all.

Can the launcher change the basket later?

No. The assets, the weights, the split and the publisher are set when the coin is created and nothing can edit them.

What does this cost?

Basket charges nothing to launch. You pay ponsfamily.com's usual trading fees and Ethereum's network costs.

After that, 20% of every settle or harvest goes to the Basket treasury and 1% to whoever pressed the button, both fixed in the program. Your own share is whatever split you chose at launch, up to 50%.
